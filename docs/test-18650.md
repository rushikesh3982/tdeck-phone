# Testing the assembled phone (18650 version)

One round of tests once the phone is assembled ([assembly-18650.md](assembly-18650.md)), in this order. Each step
says what you should see, and what to do if you don't. It takes about an hour, plus up to 15 minutes by a window for
GPS.

The tests use the [test program](../firmware/test-program/README.md): it shows the modem's state on the T-Deck's
screen and lets the Mac talk to the modem over the T-Deck's USB-C. Claude loads it, runs the Mac's side and puts
Meshtastic back afterwards. You hold the phone, make the calls and read the screen.

## What you need

- The assembled phone, its micro-SIM, and a USB-C cable to the Mac.
- Your other phone, to call and text the T-Deck phone.
- A place near a window (or outdoors) for GPS.
- The bench spot where the modem was tested on 7 Nov 2026, for the 4G antenna's reading.

The meter checks are already done during assembly (steps 4, 5 and 9 of the assembly guide).

## 1. Before anything is loaded

1. **Modem switch off** (back's right wall). Switch on the T-Deck with its own switch (the slider on the right
   side).
   - **Expected:** Meshtastic starts as usual, showing a battery level. That proves the cell, the protection board
     and the battery lead.
   - **If the T-Deck doesn't start:** plug in USB-C. If it starts on USB but not on the cell, the battery lead or the
     protection board is the problem: check the lead's + and − at the socket.
2. **Mechanical parts:** the T-Deck's switch slider, the reset button (it restarts the T-Deck), the keyboard and the
   trackball all work through the case. A microSD card clicks in and out.

## 2. Load the test program

Claude loads it over USB.
- **Expected:**
  - **two beeps** from the speaker, through the right-side grille;
  - the screen's rows appear, with Modem: "no answer: switch on?" (the modem switch is still off);
  - Battery: the cell's voltage, 3.6–4.2 V.
- **No beeps:** the speaker's lead isn't in the T-Deck's SPK socket, or the speaker has come loose from the grille.

## 3. Modem on

1. Switch the modem on (back's right wall).
   - **Expected, within about 20 s:**
     - the network light glows through the back's window;
     - Modem: "answering (A7672S-FASE)";
     - SIM: "ready".
   - **Modem stays "no answer":**
     - First check the network light. **Light on:** the modem has power; the data wires are the problem (TX to TX,
       RX to RX, straight across).
     - **Light off:** the modem gets no power. Check the switch's two wires and the C11 wire.
   - **SIM "not found":** switch the modem off, take the card out and push it back in (cut corner in last), switch
     on again.
2. Wait up to a minute.
   - **Expected:**
     - Network: "registered" with your operator;
     - VoLTE: "yes: calls go over 4G";
     - the network light changes from steady to blinking.
   - **Network stays "searching":** take the phone near a window, and check the 4G antenna's cable is in U.FL1.
3. **Switch test:** switch the modem off.
   - **Expected:** Modem: "no answer: switch on?" within a few seconds. Switch it on again, and it comes back.

## 4. Battery readings

Read the Battery row: the T-Deck's reading, with the modem's in brackets.
- **Expected:** both 3.6–4.2 V, and within about 0.1 V of each other.
- **The modem's reading is about 0.01 V** (as it was on the bench on 5 V): its own battery reading is wrong on this
  board. Then never use `AT+CPMVT` (the modem's low-battery switch-off). The phone software watches the T-Deck's
  reading instead.

## 5. 4G antenna

At the bench spot used on 7 Nov 2026, where the sticker antenna lying in the open read **−91 dBm on band 5**:
1. Put the phone down where the modem board lay that day, and leave it for a minute.
2. Read the **Average** row once it shows "last 5".
- **Band 5, and −96 dBm or better** (within 5 dB): the inside antenna is good. Note the reading.
- **Band 5, and worse than −96 dBm:** the inside antenna loses too much. Fit the 4G stubby in the top-right hole
  ([design-18650.md](design-18650.md#4g-antenna)).
- **Another band:** readings on different bands can't be compared. Go by the real use instead: in the room where you
  use the phone most, it should stay above about −100 dBm, and calls (step 6) should be clear.

## 6. Calls

Claude sends the commands from the Mac; you hold the phone to your ear like a normal phone.
1. **Outgoing call:** Claude dials your other phone (`ATD+91XXXXXXXXXX;`). Answer it.
   - **Expected:**
     - Call: "in a call, on 4G (VoLTE)";
     - you hear the other phone clearly in the earpiece;
     - the other phone hears you clearly.
   - **Keep the call going for 2 minutes:** the modem must not restart. Watch the Modem row stay "answering". A
     restart in a call means the battery side can't supply the modem's bursts: the switch, or a loose contact.
   - **Sound levels:**
     - If the earpiece is too loud or quiet, Claude changes `AT+COUTGAIN` (0–7, now 5).
     - If the other side hears you faintly, Claude changes `AT+CMICGAIN` (0–7, now 4).
   - Claude hangs up (`AT+CHUP`). Call: "call ended".
2. **Incoming call:** call the T-Deck phone from your other phone.
   - **Expected:**
     - **two beeps on every ring** from the speaker;
     - Call: "ringing".
   - Claude answers (`ATA`). Talk for a moment, then hang up on your other phone. Call: "call ended".

## 7. SMS

1. **Receive:** text the T-Deck phone from your other phone.
   - **Expected:** SMS: "new: AT+CMGR=n reads it". Claude reads it, and your text appears on the Mac.
2. **Send:** Claude sends a text to your other phone (`AT+CMGS`). It arrives.

## 8. GPS

Take the phone to a window (or outdoors), screen up, with the top edge toward the sky if you can. Leave it for up to
15 minutes.
- **Expected:** GPS: "fix, N satellites". On the bench the first fix took about 7 minutes.
- **No fix after 15 minutes outdoors:**
  - The antenna's cable may be out of U.FL2.
  - Or the T-Deck's own radio noise reaches the antenna. A note for later: try with Wi-Fi off in the phone software.

Note the time to the fix and the number of satellites (the design asks to check them with the T-Deck running).

## 9. Put Meshtastic back

1. Claude loads Meshtastic again (the phone version), over USB: only its program, not its saved settings. The
   settings stay on the T-Deck through the tests, because the test program uses the same storage layout and never
   writes to the settings' part.
2. **Expected:** Meshtastic starts with its old name and channels.
3. **Calibrate the touch screen** if taps miss or don't register. Meshtastic 2.7.26 uses a different touch driver
   on the T-Deck than 2.7.10 did, and the old calibration no longer fits. On 13 Nov 2026 touch was poor until this
   was done; afterwards it worked fine.
   1. If the screen is dim or dark, press a key or the trackball first: touch is switched off while the screen
      sleeps.
   2. Open **Settings**, the gear at the bottom of the left-hand column. If tapping misses it, roll the trackball onto
      the gear and press the trackball.
   3. Choose **Screen Calibration** and tap the marks the screen shows.
4. **Two checks the design asks for:**
   - **Bluetooth:** connect the Meshtastic app on your phone, then walk a few metres away. The cell sits behind the
     T-Deck's Wi-Fi/Bluetooth antenna, so the range may be shorter than before.
   - **LoRa:** a message reaches another node, if one is nearby.
